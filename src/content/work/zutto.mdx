<Section id="problem">
Puerto Rico's small businesses (cafés, restaurants, shops) are hard to discover. Large platforms like Google Maps and Yelp are built for tourists and chains, and bury small, independently owned spots, which can't afford ad budgets or a loyalty app of their own. A <a href="https://news.microsoft.com/es-xl/77-de-pymes-en-puerto-rico-estan-en-proceso-de-transformacion-digital/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2024 Microsoft study</a> found that customer acquisition was one of the top challenges Puerto Rico small businesses identified, named by 24% of those surveyed. Zutto set out to fix both sides of that problem: giving residents a reason to discover and return to local spots, and giving those businesses an affordable way to turn a first visit into a regular customer.
</Section>

<Section id="what-i-built">
I built a gamified local-discovery and loyalty app for Puerto Rico, a React Native mobile app covering three things: browsing and searching nearby businesses, checking in when you visit one, and earning points toward rewards from those same businesses.

<Section id="how-it-works">
Zutto Radar is the app's core feature: using your profile of habits, interests, and preferences, you search in plain language, like asking for a cold matcha without lactose, and swipe through the results to see your selection. Checking in and completing daily and weekly challenges, like visiting a new local business, earns points toward the app's loyalty rewards.
</Section>

<Section id="results">
I interviewed local businesses and general users, demoing how Zutto worked. Feedback was mixed, not a clear verdict either way: some business owners didn't see it fitting into how they run their business, others questioned the benefit over existing tools like Google Maps, and a smaller group of users said checking in on every visit was a hassle to keep up. That points to an idea that needs more research and a different approach, not one that's been ruled out, and that research needed more time than the Fase 1 program's timeline allowed.
</Section>

<Section id="next">
Zutto is on hold, not ruled out. It needs more research into the right approach for Puerto Rico before development would pick back up, and it stays on the shelf as a prototype until then.
</Section>
